I. Purpose

The guidelines promote responsible use of social media and digital platforms by VinUni students,
protect rights and safety, and provide guidance for sensitive, controversial, false, or unverified
situations.

They also encourage early identification of online communication risks so that harm to students, the
University community, and the University can be minimized.

II. Scope and definitions

The guideline covers undergraduate, graduate, and research students in full-time, exchange, transfer,
integrated, internship, and other short-term programs organized or co-organized by VinUniversity, as
well as certain students on approved leave or awaiting graduation recognition.

It applies to personal accounts in specified circumstances, University-related pages and groups, posts
or interactions related to the University community, and online communications that may affect
lawful rights/interests or the educational environment.

Covered platforms include Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Threads, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit,
Discord, Telegram, Snapchat, BeReal, Zalo, Messenger, WhatsApp, and similar services.

III. Core conduct principles

Students must use appropriate language and respect differences. Insults, threats, incitement,
discrimination, harassment, bullying, and personal attacks are prohibited.

Students should verify sources before sharing information and must not speculate, distort content,
manipulate images, or spread unverified information.

Students must not disclose another person's personal information without consent, including contact
details, room numbers, academic or disciplinary records, private communications, or identifying
information.

Students are responsible for content they post or share, including content in closed groups or under
anonymous accounts.

Students must not claim to speak on behalf of VinUni, a unit, club, or student organization without
authorization. When appropriate, personal opinions should be identified as personal views.

For complaints and personal disputes, the University recommends using official intake channels
through SAM before making the matter public on social media.

IV-VI. Situations requiring additional caution

Students should preserve confidentiality and follow host-organization rules during internships,
research, exchanges, and partner collaborations.

Early reporting is encouraged for situations involving safety, violence, harassment, bullying,
discrimination, disclosure of personal/confidential information, serious allegations, rapidly spreading
unverified information, media involvement, or widespread calls for attacks or boycotts.

Evidence such as links, screenshots, posting times, account names, and relevant exchanges should
be kept when reporting an incident to SAM.

VII-X. Support and review

Students affected by online harassment or disclosure of private content are advised to avoid
escalating the exchange, use platform blocking/reporting tools, preserve evidence, and seek
University support.

The University states that matters are reviewed based on available evidence, with confidentiality, an
opportunity for the concerned student to explain, proportionality, and protection for good-faith
reporters.


SAM is the primary contact point and may coordinate with Security, Communications, Academic
Advising, or disciplinary units depending on the matter.
